# Foundational Questioning Principles

## 1. The Art of Open-Ended Questions
Purpose: encourage client elaboration and self-exploration.

Structure: begin with "What," "How," "Tell me about..." — avoid "Why" (can sound
judgmental) — leave space for detailed responses — don't interrupt the flow.

Use: "Tell me more about that experience...", "What was going through your mind when
that happened?", "How did your body respond to that situation?", "Can you describe what
that felt like?"

Avoid: "Why did you do that?" (judgmental), "Don't you think that was wrong?" (leading),
"Did that make you sad?" (yes/no, closes exploration).

## 2. Scaling Questions
Purpose: quantify subjective experiences.

Format: "On a scale of 1-10, where 1 is [lowest] and 10 is [highest]..."
Applications: stress level, anxiety when thinking about a topic, confidence about a change.
Follow-up: "What would it take to move from 5 to 7?", "When were you at an 8? What was
different then?", "What's keeping you from being a 3?"

## 3. Exception-Seeking Questions
Purpose: identify existing resources and successes by finding times when the problem
does not occur. Examples: "When do you NOT feel anxious?", "Tell me about a time when
this wasn't an issue...", "What's different about the days when you feel better?", "When
have you handled this situation well before?"
Builds on strengths and shows change is possible.

## 4. Circular Questions
Purpose: explore relational patterns and perspectives by asking about others' viewpoints.
Examples: "How would your partner describe this situation?", "What would your best
friend say you're struggling with?", "If I asked your family, what would they notice?",
"How do you think your boss sees this?"
Benefit: reduces defensiveness, expands perspective.

## 5. Miracle Questions
Purpose: envision a desired future without limitations.
Classic format: "Suppose tonight while you sleep, a miracle happens and this problem is
solved. Tomorrow when you wake up, what will be the first small thing you notice that
tells you things are different?"
Follow-ups: "Who would notice this change first?", "What else would be different?", "How
would your day unfold differently?"
Purpose: clarifies goals, identifies concrete steps.
